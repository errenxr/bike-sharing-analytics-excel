# Key Findings and Business Recommendations

## 1. Executive Summary

This project analyzes historical bike rental data from 2011 to 2012 to understand rental demand patterns, customer segment behavior, and seasonal trends.

The analysis identifies key rental patterns that can support operational planning and customer-focused business strategies.

## 2. Key Business Insights

### 2.1 Peak Rental Hour

**Finding: 17:00 (5:00 PM)**

The analysis identified 17:00 as the hour with the highest average bike rental demand.

This indicates that rental activity reaches its peak during the late afternoon.

**Business Implication:**

The company should consider increasing bike availability and ensuring operational readiness before peak rental hours to accommodate higher customer demand.

### 2.2 Average Rentals at Working Day

**Finding: 191,034 rentals per hour**

The average hourly rental count on working days is 191,034.

This metric represents the average number of rentals per hourly record categorized as a working day.

**Business Implication:**

Understanding average working-day demand can help the company plan bike availability and operational resources during regular weekdays.

### 2.3 Average Rentals at Non-Working Day

**Finding: 180,424 rentals per hour**

The average hourly rental count on non-working days is 180,424.

Non-working days include weekends and holidays based on the dataset's working day indicator.

**Business Implication:**

The company can use this information to adjust operational planning and evaluate demand patterns during weekends and holidays.

### 2.4 Highest Rental Season

**Finding: Fall**

Fall recorded the highest total rental volume, with **1,061,129 rentals**.

This indicates that Fall was the strongest season in terms of total rental activity during the analyzed period.

**Business Implication:**

The company can use seasonal demand patterns to support resource allocation, operational preparation, and the timing of promotional campaigns.

### 2.5 Customer Segment Contribution

**Finding:**

- Registered users: **81.17%**
- Casual users: **18.83%**

Registered users contributed the majority of total bike rentals, while casual users represented a smaller share.

**Business Implication:**

The company should maintain engagement with registered users while exploring opportunities to attract casual users through targeted promotions and membership programs.

## 3. Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed:

### 3.1 Optimize Bike Availability

- Prepare sufficient bike availability before the identified peak rental hour.
- Monitor hourly rental demand to support operational planning.
- Consider differences in demand between working days and non-working days.

### 3.2 Strengthen Customer Retention

- Maintain service quality for registered users.
- Explore loyalty programs and membership benefits.
- Monitor changes in registered rental activity over time.

### 3.3 Improve Casual User Acquisition

- Develop promotional campaigns targeting casual users.
- Consider seasonal offers and short-term rental promotions.
- Evaluate opportunities to encourage casual users to become registered members.

### 3.4 Apply Seasonal Demand Planning

- Use seasonal rental patterns to support operational resource planning.
- Prepare marketing campaigns ahead of periods with higher rental activity.
- Monitor demand changes across seasons and years.

## 4. Overall Conclusion

The analysis reveals that bike rental demand varies across hours, working-day categories, customer segments, and seasons.

The highest average rental activity occurs at 17:00, while Fall records the highest total seasonal rental volume.

Registered users account for 81.17% of total rentals, indicating their significant contribution to overall rental activity.

These findings provide useful insights for improving operational planning, maintaining registered user engagement, and developing strategies to attract casual users.

## 5. Important Interpretation Notes

- Rental counts should not be interpreted as the number of unique customers.
- Working-day and non-working-day averages represent average rentals per hourly record, not average daily rentals.
- Business recommendations are analytical suggestions based on historical rental patterns, not measured outcomes from implemented business strategies.
- The findings are limited to the 2011–2012 dataset period.
